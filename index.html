I'm looking for a young actor to play the lead.

The film

Scooterland is about Parker, a boy of about four whose behaviour doesn't fit anything the adults around him expect. His parents cycle through daycare, doctors, babysitters and assessment forms, none of which explain him. The film doesn't give Parker a diagnosis — not to avoid the subject, but because that's how it actually goes: the systems are slow, contradictory, and frequently more interested in a category than in the child in front of them. Parker eventually leaves on his scooter, and the film opens outward into an adventure through the neighbourhood, increasingly seen from inside his own sensory experience.

The role

Parker is the lead. I'm looking for a child of five or six who can play four — someone comfortable with direction, with repetition, with strangers, and with imaginative play. No particular background is needed, and no experience of neurodivergence is needed or expected. What matters most is a family able to make a real commitment.

The commitment is genuine, but gentler than a conventional shoot: short days spread across summer 2027, a unit of two or three people, and a parent present throughout. No child is asked to sustain a feature-length dramatic performance. Compensation is grant-dependent — an honorarium for the child's time, with details set once the production budget is finalised.

If this sounds like your family, write below. I'd want to walk anyone interested through all of it properly before anything was agreed.

How the film is made

Most of Scooterland is built from 35mm still photographs rather than filmed scenes. Those photographs are the source material the film's moving images are generated from, and they're also finished works in their own right, intended for exhibition. A single strong still needs a fraction of the infrastructure of a motion-picture shot. Whether it can become a credible building block for a moving film is the part of this I most want to find out.

Other collaborators

I'm also looking for a cinematographer or photographer, a composer or sound designer, an editor, a producer, and two adult leads to play Parker's parents. Direct experience of this world — as a parent, as a neurodivergent person, or through your work — is a genuine asset, and it's expected to change the screenplay, not just inform the performances. If you don't see yourself in that list but the project interests you, write anyway.
